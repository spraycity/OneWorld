# ONEWORLD Community Governance — Draft 0.1

## 1. Purpose

Governance exists to protect the mission: free, accessible, privacy-respecting communication across languages. It must prevent unilateral capture by a company, funder, founder, or small group.

## 2. Current status

This is a proposed governance framework for an early-stage software project. It does not create a legal nonprofit, charitable status, membership rights, fiduciary duties, or a registered foundation. Those require a separate legal process and jurisdiction-specific advice.

## 3. Values and reserved principles

The following are mission-protecting principles:
- core functionality must remain available without a mandatory fee;
- no advertising in the core application;
- no sale of personal data or conversation content;
- no collection or retention of microphone audio by default;
- no mandatory account for ordinary translation use;
- no covert remote processing;
- public source code and transparent licensing;
- meaningful inclusion of low-resource language communities.

Changing a reserved principle requires:
1. a public proposal explaining the reason and impact;
2. at least 30 days for community discussion;
3. a privacy, accessibility, and language-equity impact assessment;
4. a two-thirds approval of votes cast by the recognized community electorate;
5. a public decision record and migration plan.

## 4. Roles

### Contributors
Anyone contributing code, documentation, testing, translation review, design, or community work.

### Maintainers
Trusted contributors responsible for repository health, code review, releases, and issue triage. Maintainers must publish conflicts of interest.

### Community Council (proposed)
A representative body intended to include:
- users and accessibility advocates;
- language-community representatives;
- developers and maintainers;
- privacy/security expertise.

No council is considered established until its membership, selection process, term limits, and accountability rules are publicly adopted.

## 5. Decision process

- Routine technical changes: maintainer review and public pull request.
- Architecture changes: public design proposal (RFC) and documented review.
- Privacy, licensing, data use, monetization, or mission changes: reserved-principle process above.
- Emergency security fixes: maintainers may act quickly, followed by a public post-incident explanation that does not expose exploit details prematurely.

Seek consensus first. If consensus is not possible, use a transparent vote with a published electorate, quorum, voting period, and result. Do not invent or retroactively change voting rules for a specific proposal.

## 6. Conflicts of interest

Decision-makers must disclose financial, employment, family, or organizational interests relevant to a proposal and recuse themselves where impartiality is reasonably in doubt.

## 7. Funding

Publish funding sources, material restrictions, spending summaries, and conflicts. Funding must not buy control over user data, core product access, language priorities, or governance outcomes.

## 8. Forking and continuity

The project should remain forkable under its open-source license. Backups, release signing, multiple maintainers, and documented build/release procedures reduce single-person dependency.

## 9. Amendments

All governance amendments require a public proposal and a recorded decision. This draft should be reviewed with the early contributor community and legal counsel before it is represented as binding organizational governance.
